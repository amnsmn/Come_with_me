the problem

Generating an itinerary is the easy part

Anyone can produce a plausible seven-day plan. The work that actually decides whether a trip goes well happens after that — and it's the part every tool abandons.

It rots on contact

A guide written in April is wrong by August. Rain arrives, a restaurant closes Mondays, a rental branch shuts at noon on the Saturday you need it.

The details live in your inbox

Branch hours, cancellation deadlines, seat numbers, the exact address. No form asks for them. Every confirmation email already has them.

Regeneration destroys trust

Rebuild the whole plan to fix one day and nothing is recognisable. People stop believing the output and go back to a notes app.

the loop · five screens, all clickable

Build it, watch it, live it

Each screen is a working prototype built around one real trip: Vancouver to Toronto, Montréal, Québec City and Niagara, nine days, two adults and a fourteen-year-old.

Tell it the shape of the trip

Dates and stops get you a draft. Then the question that actually matters — what do you want out of this — in your own words, not thirty checkboxes.

Let the people who live there weigh in

Share the days a local friend is driving. They suggest swaps, flag the queue nobody warns you about, offer a lift. You decide what lands.

Watch what changes

Weather, opening hours, prices and cancellation windows, checked against your booked plan. Every card shows the old value, the new one, and one decision.

See exactly what it did to your day

Accepted changes rewrite one day in place, with editor's marks showing what went and what came. Put any of it back. Nothing is regenerated behind your back.

Open it standing in the rain

One card: what you're doing now, why it's worth it, how far. Plus what changed overnight, and the contingency still being watched in the background.
why it holds up

Three things competitors don't do

Not features — the reasons the output is worth trusting twice.

It reads your confirmations

Drop in the rental agreement and it finds the noon Saturday close, the no-after-hours return, the $150 damage admin fee, the spouse who's free but must be at the counter. A form would never ask.

It changes one day, not the plan

Surgical edits with a visible history and a revert. The guide you read last week is still the guide you're reading — minus the part that stopped being true.

It knows a good day from a full one

Park the car and take the metro, because the hotel is on Champ-de-Mars. Do the islands Saturday, because that's the dry day. Judgment, not a list of attractions.
